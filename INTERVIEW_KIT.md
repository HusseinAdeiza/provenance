# Interview Kit — validating the hold-silence problem

For the HackCanton submission's validation evidence. The coach asked for real
interviews; this is how to run them so the output is usable and honest.

**The one rule that matters:** capture what actually happened to real people.
Never invent a quote, never inflate a number, never write down a pain you were
told to expect. If you can only reach one person, submit one person's real
answers — three real interviews beat ten imagined ones, and one real interview
beats zero. This is the same discipline the product is built on.

---

## Whom — 3 target profiles

| # | Who | Why them | Where to find one |
|---|-----|----------|-------------------|
| 1 | **A freelancer** paid via Upwork/Fiverr/PayPal/Wise whose payout was held or stuck "pending" | This is the *holder* — the person whose money froze | Your own network; you've likely been one or paid one |
| 2 | **Someone who pays others** — agency owner, small shop lead, team/community payout handler | This is "Amina," the core persona: answers "where's my money?" for a roster | Dev/design agencies, DAO or community treasurers |
| 3 | **A developer** who's built against a payments API (PayPal, Stripe, Paystack, Flutterwave) | Confirms the technical truth: holds arrive as status, no reason | Web3/dev groups; anyone who's done payment webhooks |

Fast sourcing: personal contacts first → Nigerian freelancer WhatsApp/Telegram
groups → X `#freelanceNigeria` → people who've posted publicly about a
PayPal/Paystack/Flutterwave hold (searchable; they complain in the open).

**Format:** a WhatsApp voice-note chat is fine. You do NOT need a formal call.
Lower friction = it actually happens inside the coach's 48-hour window.

---

## What you're interviewing on

**One specific past event:** the last time money they were owed — or were paying
out — got frozen/held/pending. Past behaviour only. You are testing whether the
*silence* (no stated reason) is the real pain, and what the guessing costs.

You are **not** pitching Provenance. If you describe the product, you've turned
an interview into a sales call and the evidence is worthless.

---

## The 5 questions (ask verbatim, in this order)

1. Walk me through the last time a payment you were owed got held — from when
   you found out to when it moved. What did you do first?
2. What *exactly* did the app or notification say? What were you left guessing about?
3. Who did you contact, and how long did each round-trip take?
4. What did you try that turned out to be the *wrong* move — and how did you
   learn it was wrong?
5. What did that hold cost you — days, fees, missed obligations?

### Not allowed
- No pitching or even naming the product
- No selling, no asking for commitment
- No leading questions ("wouldn't it be better if…?") — open questions only
- No hypotheticals about solutions — past events only

---

## What to capture (this is the evidence)

For each person, log:

```
Interviewee: [role, e.g. "freelance dev, paid via Upwork"] — [initials/alias, with permission]
Date: 
Platform where the hold happened: [PayPal / Paystack / Upwork / Wise / …]

The event (their words):
- What froze: $____ / how long it was held: ____ days
- What the notification literally said: "____________________"
- What they guessed the reason was: ____________________
- What they did first: ____________________
- The WRONG move they made + how they found out: ____________________
- What it cost (days / fees / missed obligation / stress): ____________________

Best verbatim quote (ask permission to use): "____________________"

Did they distinguish "no reason given" from "a reason I didn't understand"?  
( this is the exact gap Provenance targets — note if they did or didn't )
```

The most valuable lines are: **the literal notification wording**, **the number
of days/money**, and **the wrong move**. Those are concrete, checkable, and they
map directly onto your product claim.

---

## How the answers become submission evidence

- **Problem / ICP sections:** swap "Amina" abstract persona details for real
  ones — a real platform, a real number of days, a real quote.
- **Validation (Stage 3):** "3 interviews conducted [dates]; common pattern:
  [X of 3 guessed wrong and it cost them days]. Representative quote: '…'."
- **If a pattern holds across all 3** (e.g. nobody could tell "no reason" from
  "a reason I don't get"), say so — that IS your product thesis, validated by
  strangers rather than asserted by you.
- **If the interviews contradict your assumption**, record that too and adjust.
  A founder who reports "I talked to 3 people and one said the reason WAS shown,
  here's what that changes" is more credible than one whose validation
  suspiciously confirms everything.

---

## If you can't reach 3 in time

Say so honestly in the submission: "2 interviews completed, 1 scheduled." Do not
pad. The coach explicitly praised "evidence, not hopes" — a smaller real number
with specifics scores higher than a round number you can't back.
